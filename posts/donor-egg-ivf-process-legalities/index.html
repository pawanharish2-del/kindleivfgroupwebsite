Donor Egg IVF: Process, Legalities, and Success Rates in India (2026)

For women facing diminished ovarian reserve, premature ovarian failure, or multiple unsuccessful standard IVF cycles, Donor Egg IVF offers a highly successful pathway to experience the joys of pregnancy and parenthood.

This detailed guide breaks down the clinical steps, legal frameworks under India's ART Act, and success rates at Kindle Womb IVF in Jaipur.

The Medical Process of Donor Egg IVF

Donor Egg IVF involves fertilizing eggs retrieved from a healthy, young, screened donor with the male partner's (or donor's) sperm in our advanced embryology laboratory.

First, the donor undergoes controlled ovarian stimulation. Concurrently, the intended mother receives hormonal preparation to prime her uterine lining for transfer. Once mature eggs are retrieved from the donor, they are fertilized using standard IVF or ICSI. The resulting healthy embryo is then transferred to the intended mother's uterus.

Legalities Under the ART (Regulation) Act, 2021

In India, egg donation is strictly regulated to protect all parties. Key legal guidelines under the Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 include:

  • Registration: All donors must be registered through government-approved ART banks.
  • Altruistic Basis: Commercial egg donation is prohibited; donation must be on an altruistic basis with proper insurance coverage for the donor.
  • Anonymity: The identity of the donor remains strictly confidential to protect the privacy of the child and the family.
  • Age Limit: Oocyte donors must be between 23 to 35 years of age.

Success Rates of Donor Egg IVF

Since the eggs are sourced from young donors with excellent ovarian reserves, Donor Egg IVF yields the highest success rates among all fertility treatments, often ranging between 60% to 75% per transfer.
