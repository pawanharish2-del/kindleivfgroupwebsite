Donor Eggs: A Pathway to Motherhood

Why Use an Egg Donor?

The most common reason couples choose egg donation is diminished ovarian reserve or poor egg quality due to advanced maternal age, or premature ovarian failure. Utilizing eggs from a young, healthy donor significantly increases the chance of a successful pregnancy.

Who Needs Egg Donation?

Advanced Maternal Age (especially over 40) or multiple failed IVF cycles.
Premature Ovarian Failure or Ovaries resistant to hormonal stimulation.
Ovaries damaged by chemotherapy or radiotherapy.
A woman born without functioning ovaries (e.g., Turner’s syndrome).
High risk of passing on genetic disorders to the offspring.
Previous unexplained failures to achieve fertilization.
Single men or male same-sex couples (via surrogacy).
Women whose eggs are otherwise compromised.

Donor Selection & Requirements

Donor eggs are sourced from healthy young women, typically between 23 and 35 years old (as per national guidelines). Donors undergo rigorous psychological, medical, and genetic screening, including a thorough family history review, physical exam, and ovarian reserve assessment. Anonymity is maintained between the donor and the recipient couple, as mandated by law.

The Egg Donor IVF Process

01

Ovarian Stimulation (Donor)

After donor selection, the process begins on Day 2 or 3 of the donor's cycle. Daily FSH/HMG injections are given for 10-12 days to stimulate multiple follicle growth. Our fertility expert monitors development closely using ultrasound and blood tests to ensure safety and optimal egg quality.

02

Transvaginal Oocyte Retrieval (Donor)

Once follicles mature, a final 'trigger' injection is given. After 34-36 hours, the eggs are collected using a minor procedure under general anesthesia at the Kindle Womb IVF unit. The mature eggs are then swiftly transferred to the laboratory.

03

Sperm Collection (Recipient Partner)

On the same day of egg retrieval, a fresh sperm sample is collected from the male partner. If necessary (e.g., azoospermia), sperm can be extracted surgically. The sample is processed to select the healthiest and most motile sperm.

04

Fertilization via ICSI

The donor eggs are fertilized using the recipient partner's sperm, typically using ICSI (Intracytoplasmic Sperm Injection), where a single healthy sperm is injected directly into each mature egg. The resulting embryos are observed as they develop in the incubator for 2 to 6 days.

05

Blastocyst Culture & Endometrial Prep

The embryos are cultured until they reach the Blastocyst stage (Day 5/6), which helps select the most viable embryos. Simultaneously, the recipient mother's uterine lining is prepared using medication to ensure optimal receptivity for implantation.

06

Embryo Transfer (Recipient)

The best embryo(s) are transferred into the recipient female partner's uterus using a small, gentle Catheter, often guided by ultrasound. This is a crucial, painless step designed for precise placement.

07

Pregnancy Test

Following the transfer, supportive medicine is prescribed. A blood pregnancy test is conducted after the 14th day (or 2 weeks) by our experts to confirm the result and begin the next phase of maternal care.

Legal & Ethical Aspects (ART Act 2021 Compliant)

All donor services at Kindle Womb are managed under the strict guidelines of the Indian ART Act 2021 to ensure the security and rights of all parties:

  • Mandatory Anonymity: Both the donor and the recipient couple/child will remain anonymous. No attempt will be made by either party to find the other’s identity.
  • Donor Eligibility: Donor must be a married woman with at least one biological child (over 3 years old), and can only donate once in her lifetime (with a limit of 7 eggs retrieved).
  • Legal Documentation: Affidavits and informed consent forms are signed by both the donor and recipient couple.
  • Mandatory Insurance: Comprehensive medical insurance coverage for the donor against any complications arising from the retrieval procedure is mandatory and provided by the commissioning couple/clinic.

Embryo Donation: Double Gamete Solution

What is Embryo Donation?

Embryo donation, sometimes referred to as 'embryo adoption,' is the process where a couple receives a frozen embryo that was created and donated by another couple who successfully completed their family through IVF and chose to donate their excess embryos.

Who Needs Embryo Donation?

This option is ideal for couples facing untreatable infertility involving both male and female factors (requiring both donor sperm and donor eggs), or for single women where both gametes are compromised. It is often a cost-effective alternative to using separate donor eggs and donor sperm.

The Process:

The process is simpler than a fresh donor egg cycle as it bypasses the donor stimulation and retrieval phase:

  1. Donor Selection: Matching is done based on physical traits and blood group compatibility of the original donor couple.
  2. Recipient Preparation: The recipient's uterus is prepared with medication to build up the receptive endometrial lining.
  3. Thawing & Transfer: The donated embryo is thawed and transferred into the recipient's uterus using a gentle catheter.
  4. Pregnancy Test: Followed by a pregnancy test after 14 days, similar to the egg donation process.
